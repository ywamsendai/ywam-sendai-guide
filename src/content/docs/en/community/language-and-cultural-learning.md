## Summary

YWAM Sendai encourages language and cultural learning as part of faithful life and ministry in Japan. Learning Japanese and growing in cultural understanding are not only practical skills. They are also part of humility, hospitality, and long-term service.

This page explains the general approach to language and cultural learning in community life.

## Why do language and cultural learning matter?

Language and cultural learning matter because they help community members:
- communicate more respectfully
- understand people more accurately
- participate more meaningfully in daily life
- reduce unnecessary misunderstanding
- grow in humility and attentiveness
- serve more faithfully in the local context

YWAM Sendai encourages people to learn not only information about Japan, but also habits of listening, observation, and respect.

## Is learning Japanese expected?

YWAM Sendai encourages community members to grow in Japanese language ability as part of life in Japan, especially in ways that support daily interaction, hospitality, and ministry.

The level of expected language use may vary depending on role, season, and length of stay, but community members are encouraged to make sincere effort rather than remaining passive.

This may include:
- learning useful daily phrases
- practicing greetings and simple conversation
- growing in listening ability
- using Japanese where appropriate in ordinary life
- engaging with bilingual communication where possible

## Does YWAM Sendai expect fluency?

No. YWAM Sendai does not expect everyone to become fluent quickly.

What is expected is:
- willingness to learn
- humility about limitations
- effort rather than avoidance
- patience with mistakes
- respect for the people and culture of Japan

A person does not need perfect Japanese in order to begin learning well.

## How does language learning happen in community life?

Language learning often happens through ordinary participation in daily life rather than only through formal study.

This may include:
- listening during meals and conversations
- using greetings and simple phrases in the neighborhood
- learning from bilingual signage or communication
- practicing in shops, stations, and other daily settings
- receiving help from others in the community
- studying independently or with others

Language growth is usually gradual and relational.

## What is the role of cultural learning?

Cultural learning helps community members understand how to live respectfully and wisely in Japan.

This includes learning:
- local patterns of courtesy
- how public and shared spaces are used
- how communication may differ from other cultures
- how to observe before assuming
- how to respond with humility when corrected

Cultural learning is not about mastering stereotypes. It is about becoming more attentive, respectful, and teachable.

## Are mistakes acceptable?

Yes. Mistakes are a normal part of language and cultural learning.

Community members are encouraged not to withdraw in embarrassment or avoid effort because they are afraid of doing something imperfectly. A teachable attitude matters more than polished performance.

At the same time, people should be willing to receive correction and keep learning.

## Are there any useful phrases people should know?

Yes. Learning a few basic phrases can help with daily life and respectful interaction.

Examples include:

| Situation | English | Japanese | Reading |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Greeting | Good morning | おはようございます | Ohayō gozaimasu |
| Greeting | Hello / Good afternoon | こんにちは | Konnichiwa |
| Gratitude | Thank you very much | ありがとうございます | Arigatō gozaimasu |
| Request | Please give me this | これをください | Kore o kudasai |
| Request | One more time, slowly please | もう一度、ゆっくりお願いします | Mō ichido, yukkuri onegaishimasu |
| Meal | Thank you for the meal | ごちそうさまでした | Gochisōsama deshita |

These are not exhaustive, but they are a helpful starting point.

## How does this connect to mission?

Language and cultural learning support faithful presence, better listening, and more respectful relationships. They help community members serve in ways that are less isolated from the people around them.

YWAM Sendai sees this kind of learning as part of long-term discipleship and mission, not just as a practical survival skill.
