## Summary

Over time, the wider YWAM movement has been shaped by a number of historical words, visions, and covenant documents. These are often referenced within YWAM as important moments of direction, renewal, repentance, and clarification.

This page provides a brief overview for reference. These historical materials help explain some of the language and priorities found across YWAM, but they do not replace Scripture, local leadership, or the specific policies and expectations of YWAM Sendai.

## Why do these historical documents matter?

These words and covenants matter because they help explain recurring YWAM emphases such as:
- youth mobilization
- global mission
- prayer and intercession
- pioneering and innovation
- partnership with the wider Church
- Scripture access
- servant leadership
- spiritual responsibility in society

They are best understood as part of YWAM's historical memory rather than as a complete guide to current local practice.

## What are the key legacy words in YWAM history?

Several historical "legacy words" are commonly referenced in YWAM:

### The Vision of the Waves (1956)

The Vision of the Waves is often understood as one of the foundational moments in YWAM's history. It is associated with the vision of young people being sent across the nations to share the Gospel, serve others, and participate in global mission.

This legacy word helps explain YWAM's long-standing emphasis on:
- mobilizing young people
- global mission
- broad participation in ministry
- multiplication across nations and generations

### The Spheres of Influence (1975)

The Spheres of Influence teaching is associated with the idea that Christian witness should engage not only church life, but also the wider structures and cultures of society.

These spheres are commonly described as:
- family
- economy
- government
- religion
- education
- media
- arts and celebration

This legacy word helps explain why YWAM often speaks about transformation in both personal and societal terms.

### The Christian Magna Carta (1981)

The Christian Magna Carta expresses the conviction that every person should have meaningful access to the Gospel, Scripture, Christian fellowship, Christian education, and the basic necessities of life.

This legacy word reflects YWAM's concern for:
- evangelism
- Bible access
- discipleship
- mercy ministry
- human dignity
- holistic mission

### End Bible Poverty

The End Bible Poverty emphasis reflects a commitment to work with the wider body of Christ so that all people may have meaningful access to Scripture in a language and form they can understand.

This helps explain YWAM's recurring emphasis on:
- Bible translation
- Scripture distribution
- Bible engagement
- collaboration with the wider Church

## What are the key YWAM covenants?

Several covenant documents are also commonly referenced in YWAM history:

### Manila Covenant (1988)

The Manila Covenant is one of the most significant summary statements of YWAM's mission, identity, and values. It includes themes such as evangelism, training, mercy ministry, prayer, servant leadership, hospitality, accountability, partnership with the local church, and international diversity.

### Red Sea Covenant (1992)

The Red Sea Covenant reflects a strong call within YWAM toward prayer, repentance, and deeper engagement with the Muslim world.

### Nanning Covenant (2002)

The Nanning Covenant is associated with apostolic pioneering, spiritual eldership, transparency, and renewed commitment to obeying God's direction in mission.

### Jubilee Covenant (2010)

The Jubilee Covenant was framed as a personal and movement-wide recommitment during YWAM's 50th anniversary. It emphasizes love for God and neighbor, seeking the Kingdom first, serving others, taking up the cross, and fulfilling the Great Commission.

### Singapore Covenant (2014)

The Singapore Covenant is often associated with renewed emphasis on freedom to hear God, spiritual eldership, healthy relationships, youth mobilization, and resisting overly rigid organizational patterns.

### Covenant to End Bible Poverty (2014)

This covenant expresses a commitment to work with the wider body of Christ so that all people may have meaningful access to Scripture through translation, publication, distribution, education, and engagement.

## How should these materials be used?

These historical words and covenants are best understood as reference points within the wider YWAM movement.

They may help readers understand:
- the development of YWAM's global identity
- why certain themes recur in YWAM teaching and leadership language
- how YWAM has historically responded to moments of challenge or redirection

They should not be treated as a substitute for:
- Scripture
- local leadership guidance
- current YWAM foundational values
- the specific policies and expectations of YWAM Sendai
