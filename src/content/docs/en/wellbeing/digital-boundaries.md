## Summary

YWAM Sendai encourages healthy digital boundaries so that technology supports community life rather than controlling it. Phones, laptops, messaging platforms, and online media can be useful tools, but they can also fragment attention, disrupt rest, and weaken presence in relationships.

This page explains the general principles and common practical boundaries that guide digital habits at YWAM Sendai.

## Why do digital boundaries matter?

Digital boundaries matter because constant connectivity can affect:
- attention and prayer
- sleep and rest
- learning and participation
- relationships and hospitality
- emotional steadiness
- the ability to be present in community life

YWAM Sendai wants technology to serve healthy life and mission, not dominate them.

## What is the general expectation around technology use?

Community members are expected to use technology in ways that are:
- responsible
- respectful
- self-controlled
- appropriate to the setting
- supportive of shared life and learning

Technology use should not regularly interfere with:
- worship and prayer
- classes and meetings
- meals and conversation
- sleep and rest
- attentiveness to people and responsibilities

## Are there times when device use should be limited?

Yes. Device use should be limited or set aside when it would undermine the purpose of the moment.

This commonly includes:
- worship gatherings
- prayer times
- classes or teaching sessions
- mentoring conversations
- shared meals
- times set aside for rest or quiet

Specific schools or leaders may set more detailed expectations for particular settings.

## Are there any practical digital boundaries commonly used in community life?

Yes. In addition to general principles, YWAM Sendai may use practical boundaries to help protect rest, attention, and healthy communication.

These may include:
- avoiding non-emergency messages late at night
- limiting ministry-related communication during designated rest periods
- reducing screen use before sleep
- setting aside devices during shared community moments

As a general practice, non-urgent messages should normally not be sent after **21:00** unless there is a clear reason to do so.

Community members are also encouraged to reduce screen use for about **60 minutes before sleep** in order to support healthy rest.

In some settings or seasons, leaders may also establish a more intentional digital Sabbath or communication pause during times of rest.

## Why does YWAM Sendai connect digital boundaries with rest?

Digital habits strongly affect rest. Late-night screen use, constant messaging, and endless online stimulation can make it harder to sleep, recover, and remain emotionally steady.

Healthy digital boundaries help protect:
- sleep quality
- Sabbath and downtime
- mental clarity
- freedom from constant urgency

## Are students and staff expected to be constantly available online?

No. YWAM Sendai does not want community members to live as if they must always be reachable.

Responsiveness matters, but constant availability is not the goal. Healthy communication includes:
- checking important messages responsibly
- responding within reasonable expectations
- honoring times of rest
- avoiding unnecessary urgency

## What about social media?

Social media should be used with wisdom and integrity.

Community members should avoid using social media in ways that:
- distract from present responsibilities
- damage relationships
- create unnecessary comparison or emotional instability
- violate privacy or trust
- misrepresent the community or other people

## What if someone's digital habits are becoming unhealthy?

If digital habits are clearly affecting sleep, participation, relationships, or wellbeing, YWAM Sendai may address the issue through:
- conversation
- clarification of expectations
- practical boundaries
- support and accountability

The goal is not control for its own sake, but healthier life and greater freedom.
