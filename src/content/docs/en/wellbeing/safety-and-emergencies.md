## Summary

YWAM Sendai takes safety and emergency preparedness seriously. Community life, housing, travel, and ministry all involve ordinary risks, and responsible preparation helps protect people and reduce confusion when problems arise.

This page explains the general approach to safety awareness, emergency response, and practical preparedness at YWAM Sendai.

## What is expected regarding safety awareness?

Community members are expected to act with ordinary wisdom and awareness.

This includes:
- paying attention to instructions
- using facilities and equipment responsibly
- avoiding reckless behavior
- communicating concerns early
- taking local conditions seriously
- following emergency guidance when given

## What should someone do in an emergency?

In an emergency, the first priority is immediate safety.

Depending on the situation, this may include:
- moving to a safe location
- contacting emergency services
- informing the appropriate YWAM Sendai leader or contact
- following local emergency procedures
- helping others where safe and appropriate

People should not delay necessary action because they are unsure whom to inform first.

In Japan, emergency services can be reached by calling **119** for ambulance or fire emergencies.

## What kinds of situations count as emergencies?

Emergencies may include:
- serious illness or injury
- fire
- earthquake or other natural disaster
- missing person concerns
- dangerous behavior
- threats to personal safety
- major accidents during travel or ministry activity

## How does YWAM Sendai approach emergency preparedness?

YWAM Sendai encourages practical preparedness rather than panic.

Preparedness may include:
- knowing key contacts
- understanding evacuation or safety procedures
- keeping important documents accessible
- being aware of local risks
- communicating clearly during disruptions
- maintaining basic emergency supplies where appropriate

## What about earthquakes and natural disasters?

Because YWAM Sendai is in Japan, earthquake awareness is an important part of practical preparedness.

Community members should take earthquake guidance seriously and learn the relevant local response procedures.

Practical earthquake preparedness may include:
- knowing the designated evacuation process or meeting point
- keeping essential items accessible
- maintaining basic emergency supplies where required
- following any location-specific safety guidance for housing or ministry settings

## What about wildlife risks such as bears?

In some parts of the Sendai and wider Tohoku area, bears may occasionally appear, especially in quieter areas or during early morning, evening, or night.

Community members should take local wildlife warnings seriously and avoid unnecessary risk.

Basic bear-safety practices include:
- avoiding isolated or wooded areas alone when bear activity has been reported
- staying alert in quieter areas, especially at dawn, dusk, or night
- making your presence known when appropriate so as not to surprise wildlife
- not approaching, feeding, or trying to photograph a bear at close range
- leaving the area calmly and moving to a safer location if a bear is seen nearby
- informing the appropriate leader or local authority if there is a credible safety concern

## What if someone notices a safety concern before it becomes an emergency?

Safety concerns should be reported early rather than ignored.

Examples include:
- damaged equipment
- unsafe facilities
- concerning behavior
- health risks
- environmental hazards
- unclear emergency access or procedures
- local wildlife concerns

Early reporting helps prevent larger problems.
