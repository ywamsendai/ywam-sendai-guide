## Summary

Students at YWAM Sendai are expected to participate seriously in the academic and formational life of their school. Academic standards exist to support learning, responsibility, and successful completion of the program.

This page explains the general academic expectations that apply to students. Specific assignments, grading practices, and school requirements may vary by program.

This page focuses on learning-related expectations. For shared life and conduct expectations, see [Student Community Expectations](../community-expectations).

## Who is this page for?

This page is for students enrolled in YWAM Sendai schools such as DTS or DBS.

## What are academic standards?

Academic standards are the expectations that guide a student's participation in the learning process. These standards help ensure that students engage responsibly with:
- teaching sessions
- readings
- assignments
- discussions
- outreach preparation where applicable
- the overall learning environment

Academic standards are part of discipleship and formation, not only classroom performance.

## What is expected of students academically?

Students are expected to:
- attend required classes and school activities
- arrive on time and prepared
- complete assignments by the required deadlines
- participate respectfully in lectures, discussions, and group learning
- communicate early if they are struggling academically or practically
- take responsibility for their own learning and growth

## Is attendance part of academic standards?

Yes. Attendance is an important part of academic responsibility.

Students are expected to attend:
- lectures
- required school sessions
- scheduled activities connected to the program
- outreach preparation and related components where applicable

Repeated absence, lateness, or disengagement may affect a student's standing in the school and may affect successful completion.

## What if a student is sick or unable to attend?

If a student is sick or unable to attend, they should communicate with the appropriate school leader as soon as possible.

Students should not simply disappear from required activities without explanation. Clear communication is part of academic responsibility and community trust.

## Are assignments required?

Yes. Students are expected to complete the assignments required by their school.

Depending on the program, this may include:
- reading assignments
- written reflections
- reports
- presentations
- Scripture-related work
- outreach preparation tasks
- other school-specific assignments

Failure to complete required assignments may affect whether a student successfully completes the school.

## What if a student is struggling academically?

Students who are struggling should communicate early rather than waiting until the problem becomes serious.

YWAM Sendai may respond with:
- clarification of expectations
- practical support
- conversation with school leaders
- reasonable guidance for improvement

The goal is not only enforcement, but also growth and successful participation.

## Are academic standards only about grades?

No. Academic standards at YWAM Sendai are not only about grades or technical performance. They also reflect:
- faithfulness
- teachability
- responsibility
- respect for the learning environment
- willingness to engage fully in the school

## How is successful completion recorded?

YWAM Sendai schools such as DTS and DBS may be registered with the University of the Nations (UofN). Where this applies, students are normally assessed using the following completion outcomes:
- **Successful (S)**
- **Unsuccessful (U)**

Successful completion depends on satisfactory participation in the required learning, attendance, assignments, and school-related program components.

## Can failure to meet academic standards affect school completion?

Yes. Ongoing failure to meet academic expectations may affect whether a student successfully completes the school.

This may include issues such as:
- repeated absence
- repeated lateness
- failure to complete assignments
- refusal to participate in required school components
- persistent disengagement from the learning process

The exact outcome depends on the nature and seriousness of the issue, the student's response, and the policies of the specific program.
