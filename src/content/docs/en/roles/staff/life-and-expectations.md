## Summary

Long-term staff at YWAM Sendai serve within a shared community and mission environment rather than a conventional employment model. Staff life includes ministry, practical service, team participation, spiritual responsibility, and ordinary contribution to the health of the community.

This page explains the normal work pattern and shared expectations for long-term staff. Specific assignments may vary by role, season, and leadership direction, but the shared posture of staff life remains important across all roles.

## What is the staff work model?

The staff work model at YWAM Sendai is based on shared mission rather than a standard salaried job structure.

Long-term staff are self-supported missionaries who contribute to the life and work of the community through a combination of:
- ministry responsibilities
- practical service
- team participation
- discipleship and mentoring
- administration or operations
- hospitality and community care

## Are staff employees?

Long-term staff should not assume that staff service functions like ordinary employment.

Staff service at YWAM Sendai is shaped by:
- missionary self-support
- shared spiritual responsibility
- flexible ministry needs
- community participation
- a pioneering environment that may require adaptability

## What kind of work rhythm is expected?

YWAM Sendai expects long-term staff to approach ministry life with both flexibility and intentional structure.

A normal staff rhythm is described as a **30+5 model**:
- **30 hours** of core ministry and operational responsibility
- **5 hours** of personal development

Core ministry and operational responsibility may include:
- leading or supporting YWAM schools
- mentoring students or younger staff
- administration and communication
- hospitality and guest care
- practical operations and maintenance
- local ministry and outreach
- worship, prayer, and intercession
- participation in team meetings and planning

Personal development time is intended for growth areas such as:
- Japanese language study
- reading and theological reflection
- ministry training
- skill development relevant to long-term service

Personal development time should not simply become overflow time for routine administration or unfinished tasks.

## What kinds of responsibilities do staff carry?

Staff responsibilities vary, but may include:
- leading or supporting YWAM schools
- mentoring students or younger staff
- administration and communication
- hospitality and guest care
- practical operations and maintenance
- local ministry and outreach
- worship, prayer, and intercession
- participation in team meetings and planning

Some responsibilities are visible and public. Others are ordinary but essential for the health of the community.

## What spiritual posture is expected of staff?

YWAM Sendai expects staff life to flow from a real and ongoing life with God, not only from outward activity.

Staff are expected to pursue a spiritually grounded life marked by:
- prayer
- Scripture
- worship
- teachability
- repentance
- faithfulness in ordinary obedience

Ministry activity should not replace abiding in God. Staff are expected to cultivate an interior life that supports healthy service, discernment, and endurance.

## What does YWAM Sendai expect from staff in daily life?

YWAM Sendai expects long-term staff to:
- participate faithfully in community rhythms
- carry assigned responsibilities with diligence
- communicate clearly and honestly
- contribute to a healthy team culture
- remain teachable and accountable
- serve with humility
- respect shared boundaries and expectations
- support the mission of YWAM Sendai beyond personal preference alone

## How important is participation in shared rhythms?

Participation in shared rhythms is an important part of staff life. Staff are not only responsible for their individual tasks, but also for contributing to the worship, prayer, communication, and relational life of the community.

This includes:
- worship and prayer gatherings
- team meetings
- shared meals
- practical duties
- church participation
- community events
- role-specific coordination

Staff should not treat these rhythms as optional extras detached from "real work." They are part of the life and health of the mission.

## Are there expectations around meetings and team participation?

Yes. Staff are expected to participate in the meetings, rhythms, and shared spaces that are necessary for healthy communication, discernment, and teamwork.

This normally includes:
- a weekly **Core Alignment** meeting on Tuesday for logistics, financial updates, and strategic planning
- regular team meetings and planning conversations
- prayer gatherings
- community events
- role-specific coordination
- regular 1-on-1 conversations where applicable

These rhythms are part of responsible staff participation, not optional extras.

## How important are mentoring and 1-on-1s?

Mentoring and personal follow-up are an important part of staff life at YWAM Sendai.

Depending on role, staff may be expected to:
- mentor students or younger staff
- participate in regular 1-on-1 conversations
- receive pastoral support or leadership follow-up themselves
- contribute to a culture of discipleship rather than task-only ministry

A normal 1-on-1 rhythm is weekly or bi-weekly, depending on role and season.

Healthy 1-on-1s often include:
- reflection on recent life and challenges
- attention to spiritual growth and response to God
- practical next steps for faithful living and service

Mentoring is not only about solving problems. It is also about listening well, encouraging growth, and helping others walk faithfully with God.

## What is expected in confidentiality and care?

Staff should handle personal conversations, mentoring relationships, and sensitive information with maturity and care.

This includes:
- listening respectfully
- avoiding unnecessary sharing of private matters
- honoring appropriate confidentiality
- recognizing when a concern involves safety, safeguarding, or leadership responsibility

Confidentiality is important, but it does not override the responsibility to respond appropriately to situations involving harm, abuse, or serious risk.

## How important is flexibility?

Flexibility is very important. YWAM Sendai is a pioneering and community-based ministry environment, which means needs may change over time.

Staff should be prepared for:
- changing assignments
- seasonal intensity
- practical tasks outside their preferred area
- shared responsibility for the health of the base and community

Flexibility does not mean chaos. It means a willingness to serve where needed within a healthy and communicative team culture.

## What is expected in communication and conflict?

YWAM Sendai expects staff to communicate with honesty, maturity, and respect.

This includes:
- addressing concerns directly and appropriately
- avoiding unnecessary gossip or confusion
- seeking understanding before making assumptions
- receiving correction with humility
- speaking truthfully and with self-control
- taking responsibility for relational health

When conflict arises, staff are expected to pursue resolution in a timely, honest, and loving way rather than allowing resentment, avoidance, or division to grow.

## What is expected in communication about capacity?

YWAM Sendai expects staff to communicate proactively about workload, capacity, and sustainability.

If a staff member is becoming overextended, confused, or unable to carry responsibilities well, they should raise this early rather than waiting for strain to become a crisis.

Healthy communication about capacity helps protect:
- personal wellbeing
- team trust
- ministry sustainability
- the wider health of the community

## How does community life relate to staff work?

At YWAM Sendai, community life is not separate from staff work. Staff are expected to contribute not only to ministry tasks, but also to the shared life of the community.

This includes:
- worship and prayer
- meals and hospitality
- practical duties
- relational investment
- church participation
- rhythms of rest and mutual care

## What kind of cultural posture is expected?

YWAM Sendai expects long-term staff to serve in Japan with humility, respect, and a willingness to learn.

This includes:
- taking Japanese language development seriously
- growing in cultural understanding
- listening before assuming
- avoiding superiority or entitlement
- helping create a bilingual and welcoming environment where appropriate
- honoring the local context of Sendai and the wider Tohoku region

Long-term staff should not expect to serve fruitfully in Japan while remaining resistant to language and cultural learning.

## What does integrity look like in staff life?

Integrity in staff life includes consistency between public ministry, private conduct, and shared community responsibility.

Staff are expected to act with integrity in areas such as:
- relationships
- communication
- finances
- legal responsibilities
- use of time
- public representation of YWAM Sendai
- personal habits that affect health, trust, and witness

Integrity also includes honoring commitments, telling the truth, and responding responsibly when problems arise.

## Are there shared financial responsibilities in staff life?

Yes. Long-term staff are expected to contribute to agreed shared community costs as part of responsible participation in the life of the mission.

At present, this includes a monthly staff contribution of **¥6,000** for the stewardship of shared mission and community life.

If this amount is updated, staff should follow the most current guidance provided during onboarding or leadership communication.

## How are rest, leave, and long-term sustainability handled?

YWAM Sendai values long-term sustainability in mission life. Staff are expected to work faithfully, but also to honor rhythms of rest, Sabbath, and healthy limitation.

This includes the following normal patterns:

| Type | Duration | Frequency | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Annual Leave** | 10 days | Yearly | Rest and full disconnection from normal responsibilities |
| **Furlough** | 3 weeks | Yearly | Support development, home-country relationships, and renewal |
| **Sabbatical** | 3 months | Every 5-7 years | Deep rest, recovery, study, and long-term sustainability |

These rhythms should be planned responsibly and in communication with leadership. They should not be treated casually, but neither should they be postponed indefinitely in ways that damage long-term health.

## What happens if a staff member struggles with expectations?

When a staff member struggles with expectations, YWAM Sendai seeks to respond with communication, pastoral care, and accountability.

The exact response depends on the issue, but may involve:
- clarification of expectations
- supportive conversation
- practical adjustment
- leadership follow-up
- formal accountability if needed
