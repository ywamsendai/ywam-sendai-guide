## Summary

Long-term staff living in Japan are responsible for managing the legal and administrative obligations connected to their residence and daily life.

This page explains the normal areas of responsibility that staff should expect to manage while serving at YWAM Sendai. It is intended as practical guidance and does not replace official government instructions or professional advice.

## Why do legal and administrative responsibilities matter?

These responsibilities matter because long-term staff should not assume that ministry life removes the need for ordinary legal and administrative compliance.

Living in Japan responsibly includes obligations related to:
- residence registration
- address updates
- health insurance
- pension
- taxes
- resident tax
- banking
- financial recordkeeping

Ignoring these responsibilities can create legal, financial, and practical problems over time.

## How should staff understand their legal and tax situation?

At YWAM Sendai, long-term staff should understand that serving as self-supported missionaries in Japan carries real legal and tax responsibilities.

Because staff live on donations and are not salaried employees of YWAM Sendai, they are expected to manage their Japanese legal, tax, and administrative obligations in a self-managed manner rather than assuming employer-based processing.

Staff should not assume that filing only in their home country is sufficient.

## Are staff expected to file taxes in Japan?

Yes. Long-term staff living in Japan are expected to file appropriately in Japan and to take Japanese tax responsibilities seriously.

YWAM Sendai wants staff to be clear about this because missionaries sometimes assume that donations, overseas support, or home-country filing remove the need for Japanese tax filing. Staff should not make that assumption.

## Are staff expected to declare global income or overseas support in Japan?

Staff should assume that income, support, or funds connected to their life and work may need to be declared or considered in relation to Japanese tax obligations, including funds originating outside Japan.

Staff should not assume that overseas donations, support, or other global income are automatically outside Japanese reporting requirements. If a staff member believes an exception applies, that should be confirmed through official or professional guidance rather than assumed.

## Are staff expected to join health insurance and pension systems in Japan?

Yes. Long-term staff should expect to enroll in and pay into the applicable Japanese systems for residents, including health insurance and pension, unless a different lawful arrangement has been clearly confirmed.

Staff should not assume that these systems are optional simply because they are missionaries or because support comes from outside Japan.

## What other obligations should staff expect?

Long-term staff should expect to manage responsibilities such as:
- residence registration
- address updates after moving
- annual tax filing
- resident tax obligations
- health insurance payments
- pension payments
- banking arrangements
- financial recordkeeping for tax and administrative purposes

These obligations should be treated as part of responsible life in Japan, not as optional extras.

## What financial records should staff keep?

Staff should keep clear and organized records related to their financial life in Japan.

This should include:
- support received
- bank records
- major living expenses
- ministry-related expenses where relevant
- tax-related documents
- insurance or pension notices
- official correspondence

Good recordkeeping makes legal and administrative responsibilities much easier to manage.

## Should staff seek professional advice?

Yes, when needed. Tax and legal responsibilities can vary depending on nationality, residency status, support structure, and personal circumstances.

YWAM Sendai may provide practical guidance, but staff remain responsible for their own compliance and may need qualified professional advice for case-specific questions.

## What if a staff member is unsure or falling behind?

A staff member who is unsure, confused, or falling behind should act early rather than avoiding the issue.

This may include:
- reviewing official instructions
- asking for practical guidance
- gathering missing documents
- seeking professional advice
- informing leadership if the issue may affect staff life or legal standing

Delays usually make legal and administrative problems harder to resolve.
