## Summary

Because long-term staff at YWAM Sendai serve as self-supported missionaries, support development is a normal part of staff preparation and ongoing ministry life.

This page explains the general approach to building and maintaining financial support through healthy partnership, clear communication, and practical stewardship.

## What is support development?

Support development is the process of building and maintaining the financial support needed for long-term life and ministry.

At YWAM Sendai, this usually includes:
- inviting churches, friends, family, and other partners to give
- communicating calling and ministry clearly
- building long-term relationships rather than making one-time requests only
- maintaining support through regular communication and gratitude

## Is support development only about money?

No. Support development is not only about money. It is also about partnership, prayer, accountability, and shared participation in ministry.

Healthy support development helps staff:
- build a sending base
- communicate their calling clearly
- stay connected to supporters
- invite others into what God is doing in Japan
- develop long-term sustainability

## How should staff begin building support?

A helpful starting point is to identify the people and communities who already know the staff member and may be willing to pray, give, or help connect them with others.

This may include:
- family
- close friends
- church leaders
- sending churches
- former ministry relationships
- mentors and trusted supporters

Staff should begin with clarity, prayer, and practical action rather than waiting passively.

## What should staff communicate to potential supporters?

Staff should communicate clearly and honestly about:
- who they are
- why they are joining YWAM Sendai
- what kind of role or ministry they are preparing for
- why financial support is needed
- what kind of partnership they are inviting
- how people can respond

Clear communication helps supporters understand both the vision and the practical need.

## Is personal communication important?

Yes. Whenever possible, personal communication is valuable.

This may include:
- face-to-face conversations
- video calls
- phone calls
- personal messages
- support letters or digital updates

Written materials can be helpful, but support development is usually stronger when it includes real conversation and relationship.

## How should staff communicate after support begins?

Staff should communicate with supporters regularly and responsibly.

This may include:
- ministry updates
- prayer requests
- testimonies and answered prayer
- practical developments
- expressions of gratitude

Communication should not happen only when finances are low. Ongoing partnership is healthier than crisis-only contact.

## What attitude should staff have toward supporters?

Staff should treat supporters with gratitude, honesty, and respect.

This includes:
- thanking people personally where possible
- communicating truthfully
- avoiding manipulation or pressure
- honoring the trust people place in them
- remembering that supporters are partners, not merely funding sources

## What if support levels begin to weaken?

If support begins to weaken, staff should respond early rather than waiting until the situation becomes severe.

This may include:
- reconnecting with existing supporters
- broadening the support base
- improving communication rhythms
- reviewing financial needs
- speaking with leadership about the situation

Early action is usually healthier than emergency reaction.

## Are there recommended giving channels or tools?

YWAM Sendai may recommend particular giving channels, payment methods, or partner organizations depending on the staff member's country, support structure, or administrative needs.

Staff should follow the current guidance provided during onboarding rather than assuming that every method is equally suitable.

## Is support development only for the beginning of staff life?

No. Support development is often most intense before joining staff, but it usually remains an ongoing responsibility.

Long-term sustainability requires continued communication, stewardship, and relationship with supporters over time.
