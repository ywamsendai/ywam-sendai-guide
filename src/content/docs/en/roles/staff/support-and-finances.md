## Summary

Long-term staff at YWAM Sendai serve as self-supported missionaries. This means staff are responsible for developing and maintaining the financial support needed for life and service in Sendai.

This page explains the general approach to staff support and financial readiness. It does not replace specific onboarding instructions, but it does explain the normal financial expectations for long-term staff.

## What does self-supported staff mean?

Self-supported staff means that long-term staff do not join YWAM Sendai as salaried employees. Instead, they raise or maintain the financial support needed for:
- personal living expenses
- housing and daily life
- ministry participation
- relocation and travel where applicable
- visa and administrative costs where applicable
- taxes, insurance, and pension-related obligations
- other approved staff-related costs

This support may come through a combination of churches, individual supporters, family support, savings, or other approved sources.

## Is financial support required before joining staff?

Yes. Accepted staff are expected to reach the required level of financial readiness before joining the team in Sendai.

YWAM Sendai does not treat support raising as an optional extra after arrival. Financial readiness is part of responsible preparation for long-term service.

## Does financial readiness affect visa timing?

Yes. In normal circumstances, YWAM Sendai expects staff to reach the required level of financial readiness before visa-related processes move forward.

This is because staff should not relocate to Japan without the practical means to live responsibly and meet their obligations. Financial readiness is part of wise preparation, not a separate issue from visa planning.

## Why is support raising part of the process?

Support raising is part of the process because YWAM Sendai is a missionary community rather than a conventional employer.

Support development also helps staff:
- build a sending base
- communicate their calling clearly
- develop long-term sustainability
- enter ministry with greater realism and responsibility

## What kinds of expenses should staff plan for?

Staff should plan for expenses such as:
- housing
- food
- transportation
- health-related costs
- communication and technology
- visa-related costs where applicable
- language study where applicable
- ministry and community participation costs
- taxes, insurance, and pension-related obligations
- emergency or transition needs

The exact financial picture varies by role, nationality, season, and personal situation.

## What financial benchmark does YWAM Sendai use?

YWAM Sendai uses practical financial readiness benchmarks to help staff understand the level of support normally needed for sustainable life in Sendai.

Current benchmark ranges are:

- **Single:** ¥140,000–¥170,000 per month
- **Married couple:** ¥240,000–¥280,000 per month
- **Family with children:** ¥320,000–¥450,000+ per month

These ranges are intended to reflect realistic living costs and ordinary obligations in Japan. They are not luxury budgets, and staff should not assume they can relocate sustainably on much less without specific approval.

YWAM Sendai may adjust these benchmarks over time. Staff should follow the most current guidance given during onboarding.

## What attitude should staff have toward finances?

YWAM Sendai expects staff to approach finances with:
- honesty
- responsibility
- realism
- communication
- faith
- stewardship

Financial faith does not mean avoiding planning. Wise preparation is part of faithful service.

## What if a staff member is struggling financially?

A staff member who is struggling financially should communicate early rather than hiding the problem.

Depending on the situation, this may involve:
- support review
- practical planning
- leadership conversation
- adjustment of timing or expectations
- additional support development

Ignoring financial instability can create serious strain for both the staff member and the wider community.

## Are there finance-related expectations after arrival?

Yes. Staff are expected to continue handling finances responsibly after arrival.

This includes:
- maintaining support levels
- communicating about significant changes
- following agreed finance processes
- paying required personal or community-related costs on time
- practicing sustainable stewardship
- preparing for legal and administrative obligations connected to life in Japan
