## Summary

For many long-term staff, the visa process begins with preparation for a Certificate of Eligibility (CoE) application in Japan, followed by a visa application at a Japanese embassy or consulate.

This page explains the usual process and the kinds of documents that may be required. Exact requirements may vary by case.

## When does the CoE process begin?

The CoE process begins only after the person has been accepted through the staff onboarding process and has reached the required level of financial readiness.

YWAM Sendai does not normally move forward with visa-related processing before these conditions are in place.

## How long does the CoE process usually take?

Processing times can vary, but staff should expect the CoE process to take time and should plan conservatively.

YWAM Sendai may provide a typical timeline during onboarding, but staff should not assume that approval will happen quickly or on a guaranteed schedule.

## Should flights be booked before visa approval?

No. Staff should not book non-refundable flights or make irreversible travel commitments before the necessary visa approval is in place.

Visa issuance is not guaranteed, and travel plans should remain flexible until the process is complete.

## What is the usual process?

The usual process includes the following stages:

1. acceptance through staff onboarding
2. reaching the required financial readiness benchmark
3. completing the required visa information process
4. gathering and submitting the required documents
5. CoE application in Japan
6. waiting for immigration review
7. receiving the CoE and applying for the visa through the appropriate embassy or consulate
8. preparing for travel after approval

YWAM Sendai may provide case-specific instructions during this process.

## What documents are commonly required?

The required documents may vary, but commonly requested items include:
- passport identification page copy
- recent portrait photo in the required format
- supporting letter from the sending church or organization
- ministry training or commissioning documentation (including YWAM training)
- information about the sending organization
- dependent-related documents if family members are applying with the staff member

YWAM Sendai may request additional documents depending on the case or on immigration requirements.

## What supporting letters may be required?

A dispatch letter from the sending church, mission agency, denomination, or other sending religious organization is required.

This letter should:
- be on official letterhead
- be signed by an authorized representative
- confirm the applicant's recognized ministry status
- describe the sending organization
- confirm the purpose of religious activities in Japan
- explain the support arrangement
- state the approximate level of financial support

YWAM Sendai provides a template for this document.

See [Staff Visa Document Templates](../../../appendix/staff-visa-document-templates).

## What should staff know about document quality?

Documents should be submitted clearly, accurately, and in the requested format.

Staff should pay attention to:
- passport name consistency across documents
- photo size and recency requirements (4cm x 3cm, plain background, clear, front-facing, no hat, taken within 6 months)
- scan quality
- complete and legible files
- prompt response if additional documents are requested

Delays often happen when documents are incomplete, inconsistent, or unclear.

## What happens after the CoE is issued?

After the CoE is issued, the staff applicant normally uses it to apply for the appropriate visa through a Japanese embassy or consulate.

YWAM Sendai may provide guidance on the next step, but the applicant remains responsible for completing the embassy or consular process required in their location.

## Is entry timing important after CoE issuance?

Yes. Staff should pay close attention to any validity period connected to the CoE or visa process and should follow the instructions given at that stage.

They should not assume that approval can be left unused indefinitely.
